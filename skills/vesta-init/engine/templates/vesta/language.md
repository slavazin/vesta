# Writing rules

Read the Write card before drafting a decision, a proposal, a walk row or a lens. The sections after it
explain each line. This file changes through a `language` proposal.

## Write card

1. Write for a reader who was not here. A later session knows nothing of this one.
2. State what holds, in the present tense. Leave out how it was arrived at.
3. Every sentence is a constraint, a dated fact, or an instruction. Cut the rest.
4. Say what would make it wrong. A decision names the event that would overturn it.
5. Pair each instruction with the mistake it invites: "Do X" travels with "Don't take X as far as Y".
6. Point, don't copy. State a fact in one place; elsewhere name the place.
7. Claim only what something makes true. "Always" and "never" need a check behind them, or say "by review".
8. Anchor every fact: a path, a record id, a link, or the owner's statement with its date.
9. Write a `when` line in the words the task arrives in, not in the words of the answer.
10. Use one word for one thing. A term special to this project goes in Project terms before it is used.
11. Dates are absolute: `2026-03-14`, never "last week".
12. State the general case. Keep the instance that taught it in the Warrant.
13. Where the project itself shows a fact — a file, a setting, the code — point at it. Don't restate it:
    the project is the authority, and a copy goes stale without anyone noticing.

## A reader who was not here

A record is read months later by a session that has only the record. Words that lean on the moment of
writing mean nothing to it.

**Do:** state duties as instructions, facts in the present tense, evidence with its date.
**Don't:** strip the evidence to sound timeless. "Chosen after a long afternoon of trial and error" is a
story and goes. "On 2026-03-14 the second draft went out with the first draft's figures" is evidence and
stays.

### Tells

These phrases mark a sentence written from inside the session. Replace each as shown. The `language` check
looks for them in titles, `when` and `why` lines and Decision sections.

| Tell | Replace with |
|---|---|
| we decided, it was agreed, we chose | the decision, in the present tense |
| currently, at the moment, so far, as of now | nothing, or a dated fact |
| recently, lately, last week, the other day | the date |
| the above, as mentioned, as discussed | the id or the section name |
| for now, temporarily, to do | a revisit trigger, or nothing |
| the latest, the previous, the old one | the thing's id, name or path |

## True until something says otherwise

Each sentence in a Decision holds until a named event overturns it. Before keeping a sentence, name that
event. If none can be named and the sentence has no date, it does not belong.

A decision carries no history. It does not say when it became true or what it replaced; it says what
holds. When it stops holding it is replaced, not annotated.

**Do:** keep detail that goes stale on its own schedule — versions, counts, lists of files — out of the
Decision. Put it in the Warrant, dated.
**Don't:** delete the facts that constrain the decision to pass this test. A decision with no grounding
cannot be checked, which is worse than one that has gone stale.

## When-lines, conditions and triggers

A `when` line, a row's `fires-when` and a trigger's `if` are read by someone who does not yet know what the
record says. If the words fail to catch their eye, nothing repairs it: the record is not opened.

**Do:** write the condition as the task looks before the record has helped — the symptoms, the kind of
change, the place. "Sending anything to a client; a figure quoted from the budget."
**Don't:** write the record's conclusion. "When the figures have not been re-checked" is seen only by
someone who already knows the answer.

**Do:** lean broad. Opening a record that turns out not to apply costs a glance. A record that stays closed
costs the mistake it existed to prevent.
**Don't:** broaden past any plausible link to the record. A `when` line that catches everything teaches the reader
to skim. Narrow with `except`, naming a concrete case, instead of trimming the `when` line.

**Do:** use a word from Project terms where one fits.
**Don't:** coin a term inside a condition. Add it to Project terms first.

## Lenses

A lens is a question with a challenge attached.

**Do:** address the question to the reader, about the work in front of them, so that an answer needs a
pointer: a path, a record, a line.
**Don't:** pair it with a challenge that bounds nothing ("or was it fine?"). The challenge names the
specific way an answer goes too far.

## Reports

**Do:** report a count with the ids behind it. "Loaded 2: D-004 applies, D-009 does not bear." Say
"none matched (index read)" when that is the result.
**Don't:** report an assurance. "The relevant decisions were checked" reads the same whether or not anyone
looked.
